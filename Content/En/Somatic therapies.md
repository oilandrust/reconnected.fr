# Somatic therapies
date: 27 October 2026
datetime: 2026-10-27
kicker: Article

## What are somatic therapies?

Somatic therapies are approaches to therapy or psychotherapy that pay particular attention to the body. They focus on the link between body and psychology, with the idea that trauma leaves its imprint in the body. Among them are Somatic Experiencing, Hakomi, Focusing, Somatic IFS (Internal Family Systems), Sensorimotor Psychotherapy, some forms of EMDR, and other methods inspired by polyvagal theory.

## Where do these methods come from?

Approaches that explore the mind–body connection are not new: Freud and the psychoanalysts already observed the link between the body and the unconscious. The somatic therapy movement grew mainly in the United States and is now well established in Scandinavia, Germany, and international communities of personal development.

## Do these methods have a scientific basis?

They were developed by professionals — psychologists and therapists — with solid academic training and substantial clinical experience. They have integrated advances in neuroscience to explain their mechanisms. Their effectiveness has been shown in several comparative studies. It is not yet as widely documented as for approaches such as CBT: these methods are more recent, and far fewer researchers work on them.

## What kinds of difficulties can they help with?

They can help with many concerns: anxiety, relational difficulties, trauma (accidents, PTSD, childhood trauma, emotional neglect). For more severe situations, it is better to turn to specialised psychiatric care.

## What is the difference between Somatic Experiencing and Hakomi?

Somatic Experiencing and Hakomi are close and share many principles and interventions. Somatic Experiencing is especially specialised in shock trauma, which can show up as PTSD. Hakomi gives more attention to childhood and relational trauma.
